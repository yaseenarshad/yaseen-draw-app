YAZ-796 · Knowledge Management · The Lay of the Land

Where should a page live?

You asked whether to keep folders, go flat like Roam, or something else. This page explains the whole landscape from zero — what you have, what's wrong, what others did, and the one decision that matters.

The short answer

A folder can hold a file in only one place. Your pages belong to many places at once. That's the entire conflict, and it can't be fixed by picking better folders.

Your app was already designed around this: folders are just parking spots, and the real organization lives on the page itself. Your wiki only half-uses that design. The decision on the table is whether to commit to it fully.

I went through the whole vault. Three findings, in order of importance.

Finding 1 — The same fact is written in two places

Take the problem page "Lead Scoring." Where the file sits already says it belongs to Lead Gen → Cross-Channel. Then the index card says it again:

Part 4 The mental model

Three different jobs, one confused tool

Here's the frame that makes everything click. "Organizing knowledge" is actually three separate jobs, and every tool you named — Roam, Obsidian, Notion, Wikipedia — is a different opinion about how to split them up:

1 · Storage
"Where does the file physically sit?"
A file path. Somebody has to pick a spot on the disk — but the spot doesn't have to mean anything.  metrics/calculated/CAC.md
2 · Meaning
"What is it, and what is it connected to?"
The index card and the links. This is where "CAC is a metric, used by Meta Ads, relevant to four functions" lives. Many connections per page — a web, not a tree.  channels: ["[[Meta Ads]]"]
3 · Navigation
"How do I find it when I need it?"
The lenses you look through: the sidebar tree, search, live tables, backlinks, hub pages. There can be many lenses over the same pages — none of them is "the" organization.
A folder answers all three questions at once — with one answer. That's its original sin. It's fine for a small drawer of files; it breaks the moment one page belongs to two things. Your app already keeps the three jobs separate. The friction you're feeling is that the wiki still uses folders for job 2, and the sidebar only shows job 1.
Part 5 The landscape

How everyone else solved it

You asked "what does Wikipedia do, what's the philosophy?" Here's each system, one line each, judged against the three jobs:

Roam Research
No folders at all. Every page in one flat pool. Humans hand-build "map" pages — outlines of links — as the navigation.
Lesson: proved maps beat folders. But it never solved the table half — no way to give pages structured facts and query them. That gap is exactly what our live tables fill.
Obsidian
Folders allowed, but demoted. The community treats them as parking spots; meaning lives in links; "folder notes" bridge tree and page.
Lesson: this is where our app already stands, by design. The vault just hasn't fully committed to it.
Notion
One tree, enforced. Every page lives inside exactly one database or one parent page.
Lesson: has your dilemma worse — "which database does this go in?" is a daily Notion argument. Not the model to copy.
Tana
No files at all. Everything is a typed node in one pool, browsed entirely through live queries.
Lesson: the closest existing thing to our model — evidence that "flat + types + queries" scales. We keep one advantage: our pages stay plain files you own.
Wikipedia — the interesting one
Wikipedia runs two navigation systems in parallel, on purpose, and never uses folders:
Category pages Automatic, complete, ugly listings of every article tagged into the category. Nobody curates them; they can't miss anything.
= our live tables
"Outline of…" pages Hand-written maps: ordered, opinionated, readable guides to a topic. A human decided what matters first.
= our folder pages
Lesson: completeness and curation are different jobs. An automatic list can't tell you what matters first; a curated map can't promise it's complete. You need both — and neither one is a folder.

Part 8 Your call

The four questions, in plain terms

These are the same Q1–Q4 from my Linear comment, with the context spelled out. Q1 is the fork everything else hangs off.

Q1 · THE BIG ONE

Do we flatten the folders?

Context
Today, problem pages are filed deep inside function folders (functions/1 Lead Gen/1.1 Cross-Channel/Lead Scoring.md) — the "copy #1" from Finding 1. This question is: do we delete that copy and keep only the index-card copy?
If yes
Problems move to a flat problems/ bin; the function tree lives only on index cards; your generator scripts do the move cheaply and nothing breaks, because the app never cared about location anyway.
My lean
Yes. This is the commit moment — everything else in this page assumes it.
Q2

What makes a page a "folder page" — a rule or a habit?

Context
Your "folder pages / master pages" idea. Meta Ads.md and 1 Lead Gen.md already play this role informally. Do we make "folder page" an official page type in the app's registry, or just a convention — a template with an intro, a curated outline, and an embedded live table?
If yes (convention)
No app changes needed. We write one good template and upgrade the existing hub pages with it.
My lean
Convention first. Being a folder page is a role a page plays, not what it is — Meta Ads is still a channel.
Q3

Do we scope the "browse by connection" sidebar lens?

Context
The future-idea mockup in Part 7 — a sidebar tree built from index-card connections instead of folder paths, with click-to-open and preview. This is an app feature, so it would get its own scoped Linear issue (not built now). Also decides the guardrail on YAZ-750: filter-the-tree only.
If yes
I scope it as a future issue with options (a new table view type vs. a pinned panel in the sidebar), and YAZ-750 gets its guardrail written into the issue.
My lean
Yes, but after Q1 — the vault change is useful with or without it.
Q4

Do we clean up the index cards?

Context
Small drift found in the audit: metric pages carry both an old text field (function: "Cross-functional") and the newer link field (functions: [links]) saying the same thing; plus a few category fields stored as plain text that could be links.
If yes
A small mechanical script pass over the metric pages. Low stakes, reversible, no app changes.
My lean
Yes, whenever — housekeeping, not architecture.
